## 0. Why this exists — NEW, 2026-10-17

Underneath the product, three jobs:

1. **Keep hope alive through the hard times**
2. **Let people appreciate what they have done and achieved**
3. **Stop the overthinking and the worrying**

We have a tendency not to appreciate the battles we have won, and to overthink the ones that may never come.

A user should be able to open their Journey and **see how much they have achieved**.

### Low usage is the goal — stated publicly, not hidden

We **want** low usage. People should live their lives and open the app when a moment deserves it. This is stated openly as a value, not buried.

**Say it plainly, everywhere:** we are not trying to get users onto the app, and we are not trying to capture their time. That is not what we do. No competitor in this category can say that sentence, which is exactly why it belongs at the front of the product, the site and the pitch.

### How someone should feel leaving the app

Hopeful. Motivated. Optimistic.

Because they saw how other people went through hard times and good times, and still managed, and still prevailed, and life went on. They saw it in the memories, the reflections, and the improvement year by year.

**The design test:** nobody should leave this app feeling worse than when they opened it.

> This app takes time, like many other good things.

### The honest limit

Sometimes the feared thing does happen. The product must never promise that everything turns out fine. What it can honestly show is that people kept going either way.

## 1. Thesis

Capture a limited number of meaningful moments. Each one is sealed the instant it is taken and unlocks exactly one year later. The Journey can stay digital, be shared after unlock, or become a physical album that feels like a keepsake from another era.

The product is not about photographs. It is about what happens when time becomes part of the product.

ME → ME. ME → YOU. ME → IT.
Not attention. Intention.

---

## 2. Tone and inner stance — NEW

The tone of the app is **self-recognition, self-progression, self-awareness**.

That is the axis it competes on. Instagram is show-off and instant. This is recognition of self over time.

Everything in the product — copy, pacing, visual language, the reveal — serves that one feeling. Nothing in it should ask the user to perform.

The product does not argue against social media and does not try to convert anyone. It is offered to people who already want to share their feelings through photography and commemorate their moments.

### The core message — NEW, 2026-10-17, must be clearly communicated

**People do not see how far they have come.**

They undervalue what they have achieved. They forget the goals they actually reached. They go hard on themselves.

The product is the proof against that. You cannot argue with your own photo from a year ago, or with what you wrote by hand at the time.

Example raised: someone who lived through real hardship, now settled somewhere safer, and still does not take in the distance travelled.

This is stronger than "not attention, intention" because it is about the person, not about social media.

Candidate line: **"You've come further than you think."**

---

## 3. The ritual

Capture → Seal → Wait → Reveal → **Reflect** → Preserve → Repeat

- Photo must be taken inside the app, at the moment the moment happens
- Photos only. No video
- Sealed automatically at capture. Inclusion is never a later decision
- No quality bar: bad lighting, bad composition and ordinary days all belong
- A messy Tuesday may matter more in hindsight than a staged vacation photo
- The whole journey is sealed and unlocks once a year as a single event (resolved 2026-10-25). Photos do not open one by one
- The reward is not the photo. It is meeting who you were, through the eyes of who you are now

Two ideas stacked: delayed gratification, then delayed perspective. The second is the stronger one.

## 4. Journey types and the Commit button — REVISED 2026-10-25

### Three journey types

The single 24–48 range is replaced by three named journey types, framed as difficulty levels. The user picks one at the start of the year. *(Superseded 2026-10-25: Commit can be pressed on any day, and the journey runs 12 months from it. See §29.)* Each step halves the one above it: 48, 24, 12. *(Corrected 2026-10-27: 48 is the old range's ceiling, 24 its floor, and 12 sits below it.)*

| Type | Cadence | Photos per year | Highlights |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Explorer** — easy | 4 photos monthly | 48 | 4 |
| **Wanderer** — normal | 2 photos monthly | 24 | 2 |
| **Contemplator** — hard | 1 photo monthly | 12 | 1 |

### Highlights

A highlight is a **limited window in which you can take as many pictures as you want.** The frame limit does not apply inside it. Explorer gets four, one per season; Wanderer two; Contemplator one.

### Commit

Once the user commits to their annual journey, **they cannot change it.** Not the type, not the count. COMMIT is a strong word and it should be.

- The word is deliberate. "Commit" carries weight; "Start" and "Create" do not
- Choosing your own constraint is itself the first act of intention

### Why three types

To reach different audiences with the same ritual:

- **Explorer** — people used to instant photography, including teenagers who post constantly
- **Wanderer** — the middle ground
- **Contemplator** — deliberate disconnectors who want photos purely as commemoration

### Open points on this change

1. ~~Explorer's cadence~~ **Resolved 2026-10-25: 4 photos monthly, 48 a year.** The types are 48, 24 and 12, each step halving the one above it. *(Corrected 2026-10-27: 48 is the old 24–48 range's ceiling, 24 its floor, and 12 sits below it.)*
2. 12 was originally rejected as "too few to create a substantial family album". Contemplator is 12, and it is the hard mode on purpose. Either the album for that type is thinner by design, or the album rule changes.
3. Highlights make the yearly total **unbounded**, which cuts against the scarcity principle the product is built on. Worth deciding: is a highlight capped in photos, in hours, or in neither?
4. Targeting teenagers brings obligations an adult-only product does not have: age gating, parental consent in some jurisdictions, and stricter rules on what can be collected and shown. Worth settling early, before the data model is built.
5. Does the album layout stay two photos per page across all three types, given the volume gap?

## 5. Capture simple, engage at reveal — NEW, 2026-10-17

A deliberate split of where effort goes.

**At capture: dead simple, and strict.** One, two, three, and it is done. No tagging, no writing, no choices. The moment should never be interrupted by the app.

**No retakes.** Decided 2026-10-17. The moment is gone, and we are strict about it. However imperfect the photo is, that is the photo. Users are pushed to capture the moment, not to get it right.

The asymmetry is deliberate: **strict at capture, generous at reflect.**

> **Goes in the pitch.** This line and the no-retakes rule are front-of-deck material, not fine print. They state the product's character in one breath.

**At reveal, a year later: this is where engagement lives.** When the Journey unlocks the user:

- writes their first reflection
- adds the chapter tag and any feeling tags
- chooses visibility (private / network / public)

This answers the open tag-timing question: **tags are applied at reveal, not at capture.**

Why it works: it gives the product one real moment of engagement a year, instead of an unlock notification with nothing to do.

**Timing, resolved 2026-10-25:** the note is an **optional second step straight after capture**, not part of the three taps. The photo is taken first, then the app offers the note, and it can be skipped. Capture is never held up.

**Solved, 2026-10-17 — the handwritten note.** Then-you is not silent after all. The user writes something by hand on a sheet of A4 and photographs it. That becomes the writing on the back of the photo, exactly as in real photos and albums.

So a single photo accumulates:

1. The photo itself
2. A **handwritten** note from the year it happened — then-you's voice, in their own hand
3. A **typed** reflection from each year after that — every later self, in text

The shift from handwriting to text is itself the passage of time.

## 6. Reflect — NEW FEATURE

Every year, when a Journey unlocks, the user can leave a reflection on the previous year's photos and memories. One reflection per photo per year.

- A comment written now, on a moment from a year ago
- Each photo accumulates a stack of reflections, one per year, as years pass
- Turns a single photo into a thread of your own voice across time
- Carries the self-awareness tone directly: the same image, read differently each year

The name also connects to the yoga vocabulary below.

### Visibility — NEW

Reflections can be **public to the user's network**, in the way Instagram posts are public to followers. Accepted as the working position for now, with the note that it can be done better.

Public means **readable, not commentable**. No one can reply on your photo.

### Timing — decided 2026-10-17

**We never push anyone to write a reflection.** The push is on capturing the moment, not on reflecting.

- **One reflection per photo, per year.** That is the limit
- Plenty of time to think. No one-week pressure
- Proposed window: the slot for a photo stays open until that photo's **next anniversary**. Generous, no deadline, and still one per year. *(Superseded 2026-10-25: all photos of a journey move to their next slot together, on the journey's unlock anniversary. See §29.)*

### Sealed and stacked — decided 2026-10-18

- **No edits.** An old reflection is sealed, exactly like the photo. You never rewrite your past self
- **You add a new one instead**, on the photo's anniversary *(superseded 2026-10-25: all photos of a journey move to their next slot together, on the journey's unlock anniversary. See §29 and Functional-Spec §20 D)*
- **Nobody can comment.** The only voice on a photo is yours. Others may read a Journey; they cannot write on it

Example: *(superseded 2026-10-25: slots follow the journey's unlock anniversary, not the photo's, so these dates no longer apply. See §29 and Functional-Spec §8)*

| | |
|---|---|
| Photo taken | 7 January 2019 |
| First reflection | 7 January 2020 |
| Second reflection | 7 January 2021 |
| ... | one per anniversary, forever |

A photo becomes a column of your own voice, one layer per year, none of it revised.

### Two levels of reflection — NEW, 2026-10-18

**Reflect** works on a single photo or memory. There is also something bigger, on the whole Journey.

**The Journey overview** — a brief summary the user writes at reveal about **how their views changed** over that year. People mature; the overview is where that becomes visible. *(superseded 2026-10-25: a new overview every year on each journey, stacking, readable by the owner's network. See §29 and Functional-Spec §20 E)*

- One per Journey, per year
- Sealed like everything else, never edited
- Stacks: after five years you can read five overviews back to back and watch yourself change

Working names: **The Overview**, **Year Note**, **Letter to the Year**.

So the structure is:

| Level | What it is | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Photo | The moment, unretaken | 24–48 per year *(superseded 2026-10-25: 12, 24 or 48 by journey type. See §4)* |
| Handwritten note | Then-you's voice, on paper, photographed. Optional | once, in a skippable step just after capture |
| Reflect | A text reflection on one photo | one per photo, per year |
| Journey overview | How your views changed across the whole year | one per year |

## 7. Company philosophy: product before money — NEW, high priority

> In the world we live in now everything has become about money. Businesses do not put their product first, they put making money first. That is what has ruined many businesses.

This is not a side note. It is a founding principle and should be stated publicly, not just believed internally.

Consequences it commits us to:
- No engagement mechanics, no feed, no notification loops designed to pull people back
- No ad model. Ads would require attention, which is the thing the product rejects
- No selling user memories, and no training or data arrangements over them
- The ritual and the service come first; revenue follows from being genuinely good

## 8. Trust as a memory keeper — NEW

The hardest promise in the product is: we will still be here in a year, and in ten.

Reference point: **Veja**. People trust businesses that stayed independent and did not sell to a major corporation. Independence itself is the trust signal.

Possible commitments to explore:
- A public, stated independence position
- A stated promise never to sell the archive, in any acquisition
- Export and ownership guarantees: the user's Journey is theirs in a portable form
- The physical album as the final trust layer. Paper on a shelf cannot be shut down

## 9. Brand language: yoga and vibration — NEW

Borrow vocabulary from yoga and vibration rather than from tech and social media.

- **Reflection** — taken from yoga, now also the name of a core feature
- **Circling back** — added 2026-10-25. The circle is the product's shape: we are all in a circle, going round and round. The anniversary comes back around, the Journey repeats, and the layers are already concentric rings with the user at the core. "Circling back" is the natural phrase for returning to a moment a year later, and the circle should run through the vocabulary, the visual language and the navigation, not just the copy.
- Frequency language, for example 432, as a texture in the brand world

Caution worth recording: the claim that 432 Hz has healing or physiological benefits is not established by evidence. Safe to use as brand poetry and atmosphere, risky to state as fact in marketing.

## 10. Revenue model — NEW, simplified

**Charge the certified print shops a monthly subscription. That is it.**

- A shop pays monthly to be an official My Journey print partner
- The shop gets the foot traffic and the print work
- The user is not monetized on the printing side

### To work out
- If print shops are the only revenue line, is the app itself free, forever?
- How many shops at what monthly price covers the company?
- What does a shop get for the fee that it cannot get by just printing photos anyway: exclusivity in a city, listing in the app, the album materials, training on the ritual?
- What happens in cities with no partner shop yet? *(not now — the album phase)*

### Reopened 2026-10-17 — NEW

The third-party shop model is **up for debate**. Doing the printing ourselves is back on the table.

- **Own it:** control quality, earn on every album, serve anyone anywhere by mail, no chicken-and-egg. Lose the in-person ritual, take on printing, shipping, stock and capital.
- **Partner shops:** the ritual survives, no fulfillment burden. But coverage gaps, and only a flat monthly fee as income.
- **Possible middle:** print and ship by default; partner shops exist where there is one, for people who want the full ritual.

Reality check behind the reopening: income would be shops × monthly fee. A shop only keeps paying if it receives enough customers, which needs users in that city, which needs a shop there first.

## 11. The physical album

- Reference is 1970s–90s family albums, deliberately not a modern photo book
- Substantial enough to belong on a shelf
- Two photos per page
- Thick tactile pages, classic prints
- A thin plastic sheet laid over the photographs, like old albums
- Handwritten names and notes beside people, not tags or handles
- Write how you felt by hand, photograph the handwriting, print it on the back of or beside the photo
- Should show the real year: struggle, business trouble, ordinary days. Not only highlights

### The print-shop ritual
- After unlock, the user visits a local partner shop
- Gives the shop access details
- Photos are printed and cut in front of them
- The album is assembled there; they leave with it finished

**On the hassle — NEW:** it is a hassle, and that is deliberate. Easy come, easy go. Waiting at the shop while someone prints a year of your life is not the kind of hassle worth removing.

Not friction but a wall: with no partner shop in your city you cannot finish your Journey at all. Needs a fallback.
- Echoes dropping film off and waiting for prints
- The shop is part of the experience, not a fulfillment vendor
- Possible side effect to validate: brings work back to print shops that lost their old business

## 14. Sharing

- Not anti-sharing. Anti-instant, performative sharing
- Only possible after unlock, and as a finished story or album
- A Journey must hold its full value even if never shared

### Visibility, decided at publish time — NEW, 2026-10-17

Visibility is chosen **when the Journey is published**, not when photos are captured. The point of the timing: a year later you know how the year turned out, which you could not know at capture.

Three levels:

- **Private** — only you
- **Network** — everyone in your layers. The default
- **Public** — a deliberate choice the user makes

Consequence: the Positivity Feed is fed **only by Journeys set to public**. That pool is what strangers draw hope from, so its size matters.

#### Decided 2026-10-18 — per photo, with layered access

- **Visibility is set per photo**, not per Journey
- Tapping into someone's **profile** gives access to their Journey
- **Close friends can be granted access to both** the Journey and the individual memories. *(Superseded 2026-10-25: close friends are the innermost ring and see nothing beyond the rest of the network. See §29.)*

So three levels of access: individual photos, the Journey through the profile, and everything for the people closest to you. *(superseded 2026-10-25: see §29 and Functional-Spec §20 H)*

### Network and discovery — NEW, 2026-10-17

This revises the earlier "no feed" rule. A network now exists.

**For now:** feed-like, as on Instagram, visible to the user's network.

**The better direction, to design properly:**

- **Layers instead of followers.** The user is the **core**. People closest to them sit on the nearest layer; others sit further out. Visual reference: the Apple Watch app menu, concentric rather than a list.

#### How layers are set — SOLVED, 2026-10-17

**Layers are not chosen. They are earned by the photos.**

Closeness is calculated from **crossing Journeys**: two people who appear in each other's photos, or share the same moments, are close. Five or more shared photos in a year means a tight layer that year.

- Calculated when Journeys are **uploaded and ready to reflect on**, never during the sealed year *(superseded 2026-10-25: there is no upload step; layers are computed when a reveal is finished. See §29 and Functional-Spec §12)*
- **Recalculated every year.** Your circle moves as your life actually moved
- A follow on Instagram never expires. A layer here does

**Why the awkward part is actually a core value**

Someone who was your closest friend last year may sit further out this year. That is not a bug. It forces you to reflect on the time you had together, and on what that person taught you, good or bad.

> That was your Journey at the time, and future-you cannot do anything about it.

**The payoff**

Two people are close. Two years pass with no contact. Then in year three their Journeys cross again. That reunion is visible, and it is earned by life rather than by tapping follow.

**Two requirements**

- **No face recognition.** Decided 2026-10-17. People are tagged **by hand at reveal**, while the user is already reflecting, so capture stays three taps. It is manual work, accepted on purpose
- Crossing only works if **both** people have uploaded Journeys. If one person is not on the app, nothing crosses. *(Superseded 2026-10-25: a tag puts the tagged user on the tagger's map only, and any user counts, journey or not. Typed names of people not on the app never count. See §29.)*

**One guard against backlash**

Never show a person which layer they are on. Each user sees only their own map. Visible demotion is the one thing that would make this cruel instead of honest.
- **Effort on purpose.** It must not be as effortless as Instagram, where you scroll without thinking. Navigating to someone's Journey should take a small deliberate act.
- **News, not content.** Like the old Instagram news feed: a line saying *that user has published their Journey, go check it out*. The stream carries **announcements**, never the photographs. You choose to go and open it.

Why this matters: an announcement feed keeps the network without rebuilding the scroll. No images in the stream means nothing to consume passively.

#### What the announcement feed fires on — decided 2026-10-18

Three events only:

1. A user **launches their Journey**
2. A user **leaves a reflection**
3. A **path has crossed** with a friend

The third is the one nothing else does. Life brought two people into the same frame, and the app notices.

#### The resulting architecture — NEW, 2026-10-17

- **Feed** — announcements only. "They published their Journey."
- **Layers** — how you navigate, earned by crossing photos
- **Profile** — the destination. Where a Journey is actually read

Nothing is consumed passively. You go somewhere on purpose.

**What keeps people engaged:** visiting other people's profiles to read their Journey — mostly the good times and the hardship — to be inspired, or to appreciate a friend or family member. Combined with layers shifting each year, this is also the answer to what someone does during their own sealed year.

## 15. The Positivity Feed — NEW, 2026-10-17, core to the product's DNA

A feed you can filter by **tags of endurance and gratitude**, not by popularity.

Working tags:

- `gratification`
- `it eventually pays off`
- `hang in there`
- `thankful`

### Two kinds of tag — NEW

**Feeling tags** — how you are: `gratification`, `it eventually pays off`, `hang in there`, `thankful`

**Chapter tags** — where you are: `first business`, `first year of marriage`, `first year of college`, and any journey of that shape

Together they let someone find a person who was **exactly where they are, one year ahead**, and read what that person wrote looking back. Instagram structurally cannot do this; it only has now.

### Draft tag taxonomy — NEW, 2026-10-17 (needs validation)

Structure: every Journey carries **one chapter tag** (where you are) and **optional feeling tags** (how you are). Chapter tags are organised under the Journey relationships.

Grounded partly in the Holmes–Rahe Life Stress Inventory, a 43-item list of the life events people find hardest. Filtered to events that (a) unfold over about a year and (b) can end in hope. Not validated with users yet.

**ME → ME — my own chapter**

- first year in a new country
- first year in a new city
- first year of college or university
- first year after graduating
- first year in a new job
- first year of a career change
- first year of job hunting
- first year of retirement
- first year living alone
- first year after a separation or divorce
- first year without someone (grief)
- first year of recovery *(sensitive — handle with care)*
- first year of starting over

**ME → YOU — for someone else**

- baby's first year
- first year of marriage
- first year of parenthood
- a child's first year at school
- first year of caring for a parent
- first year apart (long distance, emigration)
- the year before the wedding

**ME → IT — for the thing we built**

- first year of a business
- the year a side project became real
- the year of making the album, film or book
- first year of a team, club or community
- the year of the build or renovation
- first year of rebuilding after a failure

**OTHERS → ME — feeling tags, the hope layer**

- hang in there
- it eventually pays off
- thankful
- gratification
- worth it
- I almost quit
- the hard part was the start *(from the military draft story)*
- **worried for nothing** — the things we worry about that never happen. Set on a Journey after it is uploaded a year later *(superseded: there is no upload step, and tags are applied at reveal. See §28 and Functional-Spec §10)*, to give someone else the confidence that it will be okay. Alternatives considered: *it never happened*, *the fear that never came*

### Decided, 2026-10-17 — categories fixed, tags free

- **Categories are fixed.** We provide them, for example *first year of marriage*. This keeps feeds findable and stops the same life event splitting into five dead feeds
- **Tags are free.** Inside a category, users write their own words
- **Popular tags surface over time.** The most-used ones become the product's own terminology, and the best can be promoted into official categories later

The language of the product comes from its users, not from a marketing meeting.

**Timing:** applied at reveal, not at capture. See section 5.

### Still open on tags
- **Tone, decided 2026-10-18:** the tone can shift with the chapter, but even through the hard times it stays **positive and hopeful**. That is the constant

### The purpose

To surround yourself with like-minded, positive people who went through hardship and stuck to their goal. The more good energy you give and receive, the better your life gets. Instagram has nothing like this; it is show-off only.

### The use case that defines it

When you are down or having a bad time, you open a tag feed and read **other people's reflections written years later on their own hard moment**. You see someone who was where you are now, and who kept going. That gives you hope not to give up and to keep pushing.

### The founding story behind it

> On the first day of military draft, a guy with a big smile told us: *"Don't worry guys. The hard part is just the first 2–3 days. Try to enjoy it as much as you can."*

This is a very important part of the product and must be clearly communicated — on the website and in the DNA of the product, not buried as a feature.

### Two directions of hope

- **OTHERS → ME** — someone else has done it, so why can't I. *(A fourth relationship, alongside ME → ME, ME → YOU, ME → IT.)*
- **ME → OLD ME** — look how far I have come, and I still have not given up. Fits any long, uncertain process that takes years, such as an immigration process or building a business.

### Why this matters structurally

This is the first mechanism that gives someone a reason to open the app **during** the sealed year. Until now there was nothing to do between capture and reveal.

### One risk to design against

A hardship feed can become its own performance: "look how I overcame." The built-in protection is that reflections are written years after the fact, on a moment that was sealed at the time. Nobody can post hardship for attention today. Keep that constraint intact.

## 16. Walking someone's path — NEW, 2026-10-18

Not checking posts. **Tracking a person's Journey from the beginning to now.**

- You move through their path in order, not across a grid
- You see the hard times they had, then read their reflection on them
- You can follow a single photo forward: the original moment, then the follow-up photo or memory that came later
- **You see where you were in their journey**

### The hope mechanism, fully formed

You find someone who went through the hardship **you are in right now** — but they went through it five years ago. You watch them come out of it. You read them being grateful for that time.

That is more convincing than any advice, because it is not advice. It is a record.

### Being in someone else's journey — the quiet payoff

> It would really mean a lot to me to see that I was in someone's journey through their hard times.

Discovering you were present in someone's hardest year, and that they kept the photo, is one of the most powerful moments the product can produce. Design for it deliberately.

### Capturing the low points — NEW, brave and needs care

The app encourages photographing not only the good times but **the times you feel lowest** — as a promise to yourself, deep down, that you will turn this around.

Then a year later you have the proof that you did.

No other product asks for this.

**How to handle it honestly:**

- It must be an **invitation, never a nudge.** The promise is made by the person, not prompted by us
- Someone at a genuine low should not be pushed by an app to document it
- The reveal of a very dark moment a year later can land hard. Visibility stays private by default, and the user chooses. *(Superseded 2026-10-25: the default is Network until the owner chooses, as in §14. See §29.)*

## 17. The path, and why someone starts today — NEW, 2026-10-18

The **timeline / path** — the visible length of someone's Journey history — may be the strongest reason to start now.

- The only thing you can never buy later is **time**
- A year you did not start is gone. There is no backfilling it
- The founder will always have the longest path. First users will have the second longest

This is honest urgency, not manufactured scarcity. And it is the one thing a copycat cannot hand anyone: a competitor can ship the feature tomorrow, but cannot give a user a path that begins in 2019.

**The flip side, answered 2026-10-18:** the premise was wrong.

**A user is not supposed to be on the app all the time.** They live their lives, and when the right moment comes, they capture it.

When they do open the feed, they are surrounded by thoughtful and achievement posts from the past, filterable by phase.

> Consequence worth stating out loud to anyone assessing this product: **low usage is correct behavior, not a retention problem.** Standard engagement metrics would read this as failure.

## 18. Go-to-market hypothesis

- 1,000 intentional disconnectors, each building a secret Journey for someone they love
- Revealed on Valentine's Day
- No paid campaign. Test whether the experience itself creates word of mouth
- The loop: the recipient is moved, the recipient builds one for someone else

## 21. The circle: identity mark, rings and the activity line — NEW, 2026-10-25, locked in

The logo is a **circle**, and the circle carries the product's identity system.

### A record, not a rank

Each year with the app adds a **ring** to the user's circle. More years means a more elaborate circle.

It is explicitly **a record, not a rank**. Tree rings: a ring tells you the age, it does not claim the tree is better.

- Nobody is scored, levelled or placed above anyone else
- Nothing is compared between users
- No leaderboard, no badge, no tier name
- A person who joined this year is not behind; their circle is simply younger

This is the condition that keeps it inside the product's own rule that nothing on a profile should signal how well you are doing.

### Where it lives

The circle sits **around the profile picture**, as its frame. It grows more sophisticated year by year.

### The activity line, twisted into the circle

During the year the app records activity in the shape of a line, in the spirit of a GitHub contributor graph.

At the end of the Journey that line is **twisted and turned into the circle** that frames the profile. The user's linear journey literally becomes a circle.

This is the visual proof of **circling back**: a straight year bent into a ring.

### The one condition on the activity graph — non-negotiable

**It only appears after the year unlocks.** Never during the sealed year.

A contribution grid that is visible while you are filling it is a streak mechanic, and streak mechanics are banned by section 7's rules (and `Functional-Spec.md` §18). Because nothing in a sealed Journey is visible anyway, the graph cannot drive behaviour. It can only record it afterwards.

## 29. Build decisions — 2026-10-25, Phase 1

Settled while defining the data model and fixtures. These close the Phase 0 "needed before the schema" list. `Functional-Spec.md` §20 carries the same decisions, and `Phase-1-Proposal.md` shows them applied.

### The journey

- **Timing.** A journey runs 12 months from its own commit date and unlocks then, as one event. Commit can be pressed on any day
- **One at a time.** The next Commit is only possible once the current journey has unlocked
- **No abandoning.** A committed journey always runs its full 12 months
- **Frames.** Each month of the journey has its own limit, counted from the commit date. Unused frames carry forward to later months of the same journey and are gone when it ends
- **Highlights.** Started by the user whenever they choose, and last 24 hours. Capped at the type's yearly quota (Explorer 48, Wanderer 24, Contemplator 12), and the photos don't count against normal frames. Explorer's "one per season" is a description, not a rule
- **Photos are never deleted.**
- **The handwritten note** can be added up to 15 minutes after capture, never after

### Reveal and after

- **Reflection slots.** All photos of a journey move to their next slot together, on the journey's unlock anniversary
- **Overviews.** A new one every year on each journey, stacking. Readable by the owner's network
- **Visibility.** Network is the default until the owner chooses. Reflections follow their photo's visibility
- **Reveal choices are final.** Visibility, people tags, chapter and feeling tags lock when the reveal is finished. If it's never finished, the photos stay Network, with no tags, no "journey launched" and no layer update
- **Tagging.** Users, or a name the owner types in. Typed names never count towards layers

### Layers

- **One-sided.** A tag puts the tagged user on the tagger's map only. The tagged person's map doesn't change, so a tag never gives the tagger access to the other person's photos
- **Any user counts**, whether or not they've ever had a journey. Private photos count
- **Three rings.** Tight is 5+ shared photos in a journey, middle 2–4, outer 1
- **Drift.** A journey with no crossing moves someone out one ring. Nobody ever drops off the map
- **Close friends** are the innermost ring. They see nothing beyond the rest of the network, and Private stays owner-only

### Sealed journeys and the feed

- **Others may know only that a sealed journey exists.**
- **"Journey launched"** goes out when the reveal is finished
- **"Reflection left"** reaches only people who can see that photo
- **"Path crossed"** goes out for every crossing

### Identity

- **One name, chosen once and kept.**

### Approved readings

These followed from the decisions above without being stated outright. All were approved on 2026-10-25.

- **Ring changes.** Someone crossed again with fewer photos goes straight to the ring that count gives. The one-ring drift only applies when they aren't tagged at all
- **Feed audience.** "Journey launched" and "path crossed" are shown to the owner's network
- **One highlight at a time.** Starting one while another is open is refused
- **Overviews are optional**, like reflections. A reveal can be finished without one
- **Each reveal choice is set once**, even before the reveal is finished
