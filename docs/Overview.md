# My Journey — Product Overview

> Export of the live doc "My Journey — Product Overview" (21 Oct 2026). The doc is the editable version; `My-Journey-Concept.md` remains the full working record with all dated decisions.

A photo app where every picture is sealed the moment it is taken and unlocks exactly one year later, so people can see how far they have come. Not attention. Intention.

## Why it exists

People do not see how far they have come. They undervalue what they achieved, forget the goals they reached, and go hard on themselves. The product is the proof against that, because you cannot argue with your own photo from a year ago.

Three jobs sit underneath everything:

1. Keep hope alive through the hard times
2. Let people appreciate what they have done and achieved
3. Stop the overthinking and the worrying

**Low usage is the goal, and we say so publicly.** We are not trying to get users onto the app, and we are not trying to capture their time. That is not what we do. No competitor in this category can say that sentence, which is why it belongs at the front of the product, the site and the pitch. Standard engagement metrics would read this product as a failure. That is correct behaviour, not a retention problem.

**How someone should feel leaving the app:** hopeful, motivated, optimistic. The design test is that nobody leaves feeling worse than when they opened it.

**The honest limit:** sometimes the feared thing does happen. The product never promises everything turns out fine. What it can honestly show is that people kept going either way.

## The product in one pass

Capture → Seal → Wait → Reveal → Reflect → Preserve → Repeat

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Commit | Pick one of three journey types (below). Press **Commit** — the word carries weight that Start or Create do not. Once committed, it cannot be changed. |
| Capture | Taken inside the app, at the moment. One, two, three, done. No tagging, no writing, no retakes. |
| Seal | Locked the instant it is taken. Joins the Journey automatically. |
| Wait | Locked exactly one year from capture. |
| Reveal | All engagement lives here: reflections, tagging people by hand, chapter and feeling tags, the year overview, visibility. |
| Preserve | Keep it in the app, or make it a physical album. |
| Repeat | A new Journey. The path grows. |

### The three journey types

| Type | Cadence | Photos per year | Highlights |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explorer — easy | 4 photos monthly | 48 | 4 |
| Wanderer — normal | 2 photos monthly | 24 | 2 |
| Contemplator — hard | 1 photo monthly | 12 | 1 |

A **highlight** is a limited window in which you can take as many pictures as you want. The frame limit does not apply inside it.

Three types exist to reach different audiences with the same ritual: Explorer for people used to instant photography, including teenagers who post constantly; Contemplator for deliberate disconnectors who want photos purely as commemoration.

Photos only, no video.

The three are framed as difficulty levels, each halving the one above. They land exactly on the old 24–48 range: 48 is its ceiling, 24 its middle, 12 its floor.

**Still open:** highlights make the yearly total unbounded, which cuts against the scarcity the product is built on. And targeting teenagers brings age-gating and consent obligations worth settling before the data model is built.

**Two ideas stacked:** delayed gratification first, then delayed perspective. The second is the stronger one. The reward is not the photo. It is meeting who you were, through the eyes of who you are now.

**Why someone starts today:** the only thing you can never buy later is time. A competitor can ship the feature tomorrow but cannot hand anyone a path that begins in 2019.

## What a photo holds

| Level | What it is | How often |
|---|---|---|
| The photo | The moment, unretaken | 24–48 per year |
| Handwritten note | Written by hand on A4, photographed. The writing on the back of the photo. Then-you's voice. | Once, at capture |
| Reflect | A typed reflection on that one photo | One per photo, per year |
| Journey overview | How the user's views changed across the whole year | One per year |

The shift from handwriting to typed text is itself the passage of time. None of it is ever edited.

Reflections stack and never change. Photo 7 Jan 2019 → reflection 7 Jan 2020 → reflection 7 Jan 2021 → forever. Nobody can comment; the only voice on a photo is the owner's. The window stays open until the next anniversary.

## The rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Strict at capture, generous at reflect | The moment deserves no interruption. The thinking deserves all the time it needs. |
| No retakes | That moment is gone. However imperfect, that is the photo. |
| No quality bar | A messy Tuesday may matter more than a staged vacation photo. |
| Sealed at capture | You cannot curate your own year after the fact. |
| No edits to reflections | You never rewrite your past self. |
| No comments from others | The only voice on a photo is yours. |
| No face recognition | Tagged by hand at reveal. Manual, on purpose. |
| No ads, ever | Ads require attention, which the product rejects. |
| No engagement mechanics | No notification loops designed to pull people back, and no feed to scroll. |
| Low usage is the goal | Stated openly as a value. |

> In the world we live in now everything has become about money. Businesses do not put their product first, they put making money first. That is what has ruined many businesses.

We are not trying to change anyone's behaviour. We serve people who already want this. That shifts the work from persuading to finding.

An earlier version of the plan named the Intentional Disconnector and the Parent as the first two targets. Superseded by launching all segments together, recorded here rather than dropped.

## The network

| Part | Its job |
|---|---|
| Feed | Announcements only. No photographs in the stream. |
| Layers | How you navigate. Earned by photos, not chosen. |
| Profile | The destination. Where a Journey is read. |

**Layers** — the user is the core, closest people nearest, like the Apple Watch menu. Closeness is calculated from crossing Journeys: appearing in each other's photos. Five or more shared photos in a year is a tight layer. Calculated at reveal, recalculated every year. Requires both people to have uploaded. Never show a person which layer they are on.

Someone who was closest last year may sit further out this year. That forces you to reflect on the time you had. Two people drift, then in year three their paths cross again — earned by life, not by tapping follow.

**The announcement feed** fires on three events only: a Journey launched, a reflection left, a path crossed.

**Walking someone's path** — track a Journey from the beginning to now, in order. See the hard times, read the reflection, follow one photo forward, and see where you were in their journey. Discovering you were in someone's hardest year is one of the most powerful moments the product can produce.

**Visibility** — private / network / public, set per photo, chosen at publish time. Profile gives access to the Journey. Close friends can be given access to everything. Public means readable, not commentable.

**Signing up** — open, anyone can join. Invitation is the main path, not a gate.

**On sharing** — the product is not anti-sharing. It is anti-instant, performative sharing. Sharing is only possible after unlock, and as a finished story or album. A Journey must hold its full value even if it is never shared.

## The Positivity Feed

Filtered by tags of endurance and gratitude, not popularity. The reason to open the app on a bad day, during your own sealed year.

When you are down, you read other people's reflections written years later on their own hard moment. You find someone who went through what you are in now, five years ago, and watch them come out of it grateful. Not advice. A record.

> On the first day of military draft, a guy with a big smile told us: "Don't worry guys. The hard part is just the first 2–3 days. Try to enjoy it as much as you can."

This belongs on the website and in the DNA of the product.

**Chapter tags** say where you are. **Feeling tags** say how you are. Together they let someone find a person exactly where they are, one year ahead.

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| Categories | Fixed, so feeds stay findable |
| Tags | Free, inside a category |
| Growth | Popular tags get promoted; the language comes from users |
| Timing | Applied at reveal |
| Tone | Shifts with the chapter, always positive and hopeful |

**ME → ME:** new country · new city · college · after graduating · new job · career change · job hunting · retirement · living alone · after separation or divorce · without someone · recovery · starting over

**ME → YOU:** baby's first year · marriage · parenthood · child's first year at school · caring for a parent · apart · the year before the wedding

**ME → IT:** a business · a side project becoming real · making the album, film or book · a team or community · the build or renovation · rebuilding after failure

**Feeling tags:** hang in there · it eventually pays off · thankful · gratification · worth it · I almost quit · the hard part was the start · worried for nothing

**Capturing the low points** — the app makes room for photographing the times you feel lowest, as a promise to yourself. An invitation, never a nudge. Someone at a genuine low should not be pushed by an app to document it.

**The risk:** a hardship feed can become its own performance. The protection is that reflections are written years later, on a moment sealed at the time. Nobody can post hardship for attention today.

## The physical album — phase 2

1970s–90s family albums, deliberately not a modern photo book. Shelf-worthy. Two photos per page. Thick tactile pages, classic prints, a thin plastic sheet over the photographs. Handwritten names beside people, never tags. The handwritten note printed on the back. Shows the real year, not only highlights.

**The print-shop ritual** — visit a local partner shop, photos printed and cut in front of you, album assembled there. It is a hassle, and that is the point. Easy come, easy go.

Not friction but a wall: no shop in your city means you cannot finish your Journey. Needs a fallback.

## Business model and defensibility

| Phase | What ships | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The app, all segments | None |
| 2 | Albums, printing, the ritual | Below |

| Option | Upside | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Own the printing | Earn on every album, serve anyone by mail | Lose the ritual; take on operations and capital |
| Partner shops | The ritual survives, no fulfillment burden | Coverage gaps, flat fee only |
| Both | Ship by default, shops where they exist | Two operations |

If shops do pay a fee, it has to buy them something they could not get by printing photos anyway: exclusivity in a city, a listing in the app, the album materials, training on the ritual.

**Trust** — the hardest promise is that we will still be here in ten years. Reference: Veja. Independence is the trust signal. A public independence position, a promise never to sell the archive, export guarantees, and the album as the final layer. Paper on a shelf cannot be shut down.

**On being copied** — they can absolutely copy the feature. Instagram runs ads and is built to make money; a business whose income depends on attention cannot be the thing that sells slowness. They can copy the feature, not the intent. And they cannot hand anyone a path that begins in 2019.

Candidate moats, all unproven: brand association with the ritual, trust through independence, attachment to a growing archive, the album ecosystem, the print-shop network, an identity that needs no feed. Patents should not be assumed; novelty and non-obviousness would have to be assessed.

The stress test the concept passes: remove sharing entirely and the product still stands, because the value is ritual, hindsight and preservation.

**Go-to-market** — 1,000 people building a secret Journey for someone they love, revealed on Valentine's Day, no paid campaign. The framing matters: not "people not on social media" but "people who stepped away from social media but still want to share meaningful moments." Other reveal occasions: birthdays, anniversaries, Mother's Day, Father's Day.

That is a long-term vision, not a first test. It asks for a year of secret capturing and returns no learning for twelve months. A short-loop first test is still open.

## Brand, identity and language

**Tone** — self-recognition, self-progression, self-awareness. Nothing should ask the user to perform.

**Identity** — we are all just passengers; this is only a journey. Names come from within, not from sounding cool. Nothing on a profile signals how you look or how well you are doing. The name could change each year, the way layers recalculate. The Native American naming reference is worth dropping; the principle is worth keeping.

**Vocabulary** — yoga and vibration rather than tech. Reflection comes from yoga. 432 works as brand texture, not as a health claim.

**Circling back** — the circle is the product's shape: we are all in a circle, going round and round. The anniversary comes back around, the Journey repeats, and the layers are already concentric rings with the user at the core. The circle should run through the vocabulary, the visual language and the navigation, not just the copy.

**The circle** — the logo is a circle, and the circle is the identity system. Each year with the app adds a **ring**, so a longer-standing user has a more elaborate circle. It is a **record, not a rank**: tree rings, where the ring tells you the age without claiming the tree is better. Nobody is scored, compared or placed above anyone, which is what keeps it inside the rule that nothing on a profile signals how well you are doing. The circle frames the profile picture.

During the year, activity is recorded as a line in the spirit of a GitHub contributor graph. At the end of the Journey that line is twisted and turned into the circle around the profile — the linear year literally bent into a ring, the visual proof of circling back.

**Non-negotiable condition:** the activity graph only appears after the year unlocks, never during the sealed year. A grid you watch yourself fill is a streak mechanic, and those are banned. Since nothing in a sealed Journey is visible, it can only ever record behaviour, not drive it.

**Naming** — My Journey (working), originally Retrogram, from Instagram being instant and retro being the opposite. "Journey" fits normal speech. "Journal" appeals too, since the past writes something for the future self. Also considered: Chapter, Almanac, Keepsake, Unfold. A coined word stays open, with a trademark check before any claim.

### One unresolved fork from the original documents

The original voice note described an upload model: take photos, then upload them all when ready, with the app announcing that the user has uploaded their journey. It also described browsing other people's past years, effectively a feed shifted back twelve months. The concept document removed both. The current direction assumes the sealed version, but neither document ever said which one won.
