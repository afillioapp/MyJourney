My Journey: Phase 2 preview

This is a preview of Phase 2, on paper, with dummy data. It is not the working app, and it has not all been decided. The colours and fonts are the art director's proposals.

It is drawn in the light theme only, as the prototype's working default (B30). This page makes no outside requests, and it has no scripts.

The ritual

A description for a new reader, not product copy.

My Journey is a photo app with one unusual rule: you cannot see the photos you take until a year has passed. Not a preview, not a number. A year later the whole year opens at once, and you meet it. Using the app very little is the goal.

You choose your year. On any day you like, you choose one of three kinds of year: Explorer (4 photos a month), Wanderer (2 a month) or Contemplator (1 a month). Nothing is picked for you. Then you press Commit, and that word is meant. You cannot change your mind, you cannot stop early, and you cannot start another year until this one has opened. Say you choose Wanderer on 14 March. Your year runs until the next 14 March.

You live it. When a moment matters, you open the app and take the picture, in three taps. There is no second go and you do not get to look at it. Bad light stays bad. The moment it is taken it is sealed away, even from you. The app then offers one more thing, which you can skip: write something by hand on a sheet of paper and photograph it. That page becomes the writing on the back of the photo, as on an old print. You have up to 15 minutes, and for now just one go at it.

Photos you do not use in a month carry over to later months, and whatever is left when the year ends is gone. If you have none left, the app refuses the picture, without saying how many you have used. Depending on the kind of year you chose, you can also open a 24-hour window in which the monthly limit is lifted: four times for an Explorer, twice for a Wanderer, once for a Contemplator.

You wait. Open the app during that year and your year looks the same on every date. It shows what you chose, the day you started and the day it opens, always as plain dates. For now that is all it shows. No pictures, no count, no countdown. Nothing is wrong. That emptiness is what you committed to. The app never reminds you to take a photo or to come back. If you do come, what there is to do is read other people's years that have already opened. All that anyone else can tell about your year is that you have one under way.

It opens. On 14 March the whole year opens at once. Nothing pings you. For now, the plan is that a line tells you your journey is ready the next time you happen to open the app. This is the one real piece of work in the year. Writing is optional, and you may leave the whole thing unfinished. For now, nothing else is required either, not even a chapter, and there is no deadline. You read your photos and the pages you wrote. You can write a typed reflection on a photo. You name, by hand, who is in each picture: someone on the app, or a name you type for someone who is not. You give the year a chapter and whatever feelings fit. You can write how you changed across the year. And for each photo you decide who may read it: only you, your friends, or anyone using the app, which for now means anyone with an account. Until you decide, a photo stays open to your friends. Whether they can read it before you have finished is an option still being compared: for now they can, and the other option is that nobody but you reads your photos until you finish. Each choice can be made only once, and for now the app says so before it saves.

When you are done, you finish. Everything you chose locks. What adds a ring to the circle around your name is an option still being compared: for now, finishing adds one, and the other option adds one for each year with the app. For now, finishing is also when the year's line of activity is bent into the circle: a straight year turned into a circle. Rings are a record, not a score. Your friends get a one-line notice that your journey is out. A line, never a picture.

Your map. When you finish, the people you named who are on the app are placed on your map: you in the middle, with rings of people around you. A name you only typed in never counts. Someone in five or more of this year's photos sits closest, two to four in the middle ring, one in the outer ring. Someone already on your map who is not in this year's photos moves out a ring, but never falls off. Only you ever see your map. Nobody is told which ring they are on.

Your friends. Your map and your friends are separate. Two people become friends only when one sends a friend request and the other accepts it. A request waits quietly in a list inside the app: nothing pings you, nothing counts requests or friends, and the app never suggests people to add. Your friends can read what you share with your friends. Someone who is only on your map, because you named them in a photo, reads only what you share with everyone. For now, anyone can send a request to anyone, a decline is never shown to the person who sent it, either friend can quietly end the friendship at any time, and you can keep a list of close friends that only you see. What close friends get beyond other friends is still being decided: for now, nothing.

The years after. You can commit to your next year as soon as this one opens, or later. And every year on 14 March, each photo of that year makes room for one more reflection. You never change what you wrote. You add to it. Nothing tells you this has happened, and you never have to. Over time one photo becomes a stack: the moment, your handwriting from that day, then your typed voice from each year after.

Other people can walk your path. They read your years from the beginning to now, in order, seeing a hard time and then what you wrote about it later. Whether someone who is in one of your photos can find they were there in your hard year is an option still being compared. For now, they can. Readers can read. They can never write. The only voice on a photo is yours.

The moments

Pictures of screens, in the order of the ritual. Nothing in them can be pressed. Every line inside a frame is the recommended line from the microcopy, word for word, and the names and dates are dummy data.

Navigation is left out of every frame: where the ways sit is open (AD-10), and their words are not written (Microcopy MQ-3). The art direction draws each mark only beside its word, so none is shown. The weeks marked on the rings are invented, because the dummy data fixes how many photos a journey holds and only a few of their dates. The highlight weeks are the fixture's own.

Explorer. Easy. 4 photos a month, 48 in the year, 4 highlights. The most room of the three.

Wanderer. Normal. 2 photos a month, 24 in the year, 2 highlights. Half the room.

Contemplator. Hard. 1 photo a month, 12 in the year, 1 highlight. Half again.

They differ only in how strict they are. None is a better year.

A highlight is 24 hours you choose, when the monthly limit does not apply, up to a year's photos for that type.

Whichever you choose is your year, and it runs for 12 months. You can't change it, and you can't step out of it.

3.2 The rings

A ring is a record, like a tree ring: "a ring tells you the age, it does not claim the tree is better" (Concept:689). What adds a ring is PROVISIONAL: B4, switchable: one ring per finished reveal by default (Proposal:65), one per year with the app as the option, on the owner's own profile only (Spec §20 WW).

Drawn in dots (Spec §20 AAAAA, 2026-11-12; the user's direction in §0). Each ring is one journey, drawn as 52 dots, one per week of that journey. There is no ring line: the continuous line with day marks, its joins and its flares are retired. The mark and the frame keep the line (§3.1).

The newest ring is next to the frame (Spec §20 AAAAA; PROVISIONAL: B34, decided). Rings are ordered by when their reveal was finished, the latest at place 1. When a reveal is finished, its ring is drawn at place 1 and every older ring moves out one place, a step finer. A ring keeps its own pattern as it moves: only its radius, dot size and ink change.

The geometry is the same under both B4 settings. Only the number of rings, n, differs, and who sees which n is set out in §3.6.

The owner sees the same weeks under both settings (R-141, R-187). Only what carries them differs (PROVISIONAL: B28):

  • Under the default, the journey's ring carries its weeks from reveal finished.
  • Under the per-year option, each finished journey's weeks go on the owner's ring for the year that holds that journey's reveal-finished date, counted from the journey's own commit date (PROVISIONAL: B86).
  • That ring is drawn only later, so until it exists the journey's flat graph stays shown to the owner. This applies under this option only, as an exception to PROVISIONAL: B31.
  • A per-year ring that holds no finished journey is a ring of even dots.
  • Two reveals finished in the same year (possible under PROVISIONAL: B3) both put their weeks on that year's ring, each from its own commit date; a week takes the larger of the two sizes. Whether that is wanted is part of AD-2.

3.3 The weeks in the ring

"The user's linear journey literally becomes a circle" (Concept:706): the journey's 52 weeks are bent round into its ring. They show in the ring when the reveal is finished (PROVISIONAL: B5; SI:178; Flows:361), never during the sealed year (Spec:187; Concept:712; R-011). The ring records capture dates only (SI OQ-7; AD-8, PROVISIONAL: B33).

A week (PROVISIONAL: B86): week w holds the journey's UTC days 7w + 1 to 7w + 7, counted from the commit date as day 1 (PROVISIONAL: B16). Week 52 also holds what is left, so it has 8 or 9 days. Every photo falls in exactly one week.

Its size (Spec §20 AAAAA (b)):

  • No photo that week: the smallest dot.
  • One or more photos: the photo dot. A week with one photo and a week with forty look the same (R-144; PROVISIONAL: B8).
  • The week a highlight opened in, when the highlight holds at least one photo: the highlight dot (PROVISIONAL: B87). Its photos taken after the week turns make the next week a photo week as usual.

Rules for the drawing [AD]:

  • Three sizes only, all in the ring's one ink. No intensity scale, no heat colours, no green.
  • No colour, size or pattern is assigned to a type.
  • All 52 dots are drawn at once. A ring never fills, sweeps or appears week by week.

What the ring still shows. How many weeks had photos.

  • An Explorer's ring is almost all photo dots. A Contemplator's has at most 13 bigger dots.
  • So the ring shows, roughly, the journey's type and how full the year was, and where the highlights fell.
  • A ring of small dots beside a busy one marks a quiet year.
  • Fullness can be read as a score, which is what a contribution graph is read for.

Under B5's default only the owner sees the sizes, after the reveal is finished (PROVISIONAL: B88). This file does not settle whether fullness is acceptable: AD-11, raised next to AD-1.

3.4 The flat graph

Between unlock and reveal finished, the owner sees the year as a straight line (Spec:187; SI:184; PROVISIONAL: B5). It is the same line, not yet bent. [AD] for the drawing:

  • Length 300, stroke 1.25, --mj-ink, round caps, in a 320 × 24 box.
  • A photo on day d rises at x = 10 + 300·(d + 0.5)/N, height 3, half-width 300·4/N, with the same cos² and max.
  • No axis, grid, month labels, totals or tooltips (R-144).

  • The owner and the network read journey dates (Spec §20 T), so for them it gives nothing new.
  • A public viewer may not read journey dates (Spec §20 T; R-152). From one dated photo they could recover the commit date.
  • Whether a public viewer gets a flat graph at all under B5's option, and if so what it may be anchored to: AD-12, provisional default PROVISIONAL: B36.

3.5 How the ring arrives

At reveal finished, the ring is there the next time the circle is drawn, complete, with no celebration (SI:185; Flows:523; PROVISIONAL: B29). Every older ring is already at its new place. Nothing moves on screen: no bend, no dots appearing one by one, no rings sliding out (AD-3). The bend geometry drawn for the retired ring line is withdrawn.

9. Texture and prints

Grain. --mj-texture-grain as a repeating background on --mj-page, and over the envelope. It is static, fixed to the page and the same at every moment. It never appears over a photograph or a print. [AD]

A print (Concept:308) [AD]:

  • Border --mj-print-border in --mj-print, corners --mj-radius-print, shadow --mj-print-shadow.
  • No hover zoom, no filter, no caption over the image.
  • A photo's quality is not graded or hidden: a dark, blurry or badly framed photo gets the same print (Spec:56).

The plastic sheet. --mj-film-sheen is laid over the photograph (Concept:309; Spec:216).

  • It is white at up to 10% (light theme) and 6% (dark theme), in a diagonal band.
  • It lightens every pixel it covers, most at the corner where the band starts. So the photograph does not look exactly as taken.
  • No pixels are changed in the stored image.

Whether a sheen over the photo is wanted at all is for the user to review, as [AD].

A handwritten note gets the same print treatment at A4 proportion, 1 : 1.414.

Frequency language. "432" as "a texture in the brand world" (Concept:271) is not used anywhere in the visual system. It would need waves or vibrating motion, and its health claim is not established (Concept:273). If the user wants it, it is a copy question. [AD]

Motion in general.

  • Only --mj-motion-page, and --mj-motion-bend if AD-3 is answered yes.
  • Nothing loops, breathes, pulses or draws the eye while the screen is idle.
  • No pull-to-refresh art (SI:51). Nothing is timed to bring anyone back (CL:70, 76).
